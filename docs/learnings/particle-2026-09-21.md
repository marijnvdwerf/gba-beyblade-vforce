### Signature and signedness evidence

`void sub_804E6A4(ParticleSystem* system, s16 scale, s16 velocity, unk16 lifetime,
unk16 timer, s16 sequence, s16 frameValue)` — seven parameters, the last three on
the stack at `sp+0x2c`, `sp+0x30`, `sp+0x34`.

- `scale`, `velocity`: `lsl #16; asr #16` before each `mul` — signed 16-bit.
- `timer`: `lsl #16; lsr #16` in the prologue then spilled with `str [sp]` and
  reloaded twice with `ldrh` — unsigned 16-bit.
- `sequence`: `lsl #16; asr #13` (sign extension fused with `* 8`) — signed 16-bit.
- `lifetime`: stored raw with `strh r3, [r7, #26]`, no extension visible. Either
  `unk16` or `s16` compiles identically (measured), `unk32` does not (see below).
  `unk16` matches the sibling emitters sub_804E594/sub_804E7D4.
- `frameValue`: the single prologue `lsl #16` is consumed twice, by `lsr #16`
  (value stored to `unk1C` and `sprite->frame.word`) and by `asr #16` (operand of
  `cmp` against `frame->unk2`). One `s16` parameter reproduces both: agbcc keeps
  the zero-extended pseudo for the halfword stores and a separate sign-extended
  pseudo for the int-promoted compare. `unk16` (75 differing instructions) and
  `unk32` lose the `asr` — `s16` is proven.
- `Particle::unk1A` stays `s16`: sub_804EA88 reads offset 26 with both
  `ldrsh r0, [r3, r2]` (r2 = 26) at 0x65e and `ldrh r4, [r3, #26]` at 0x660.

### What moved the diff: allocation priority

`.greg` sorts allocnos by `floor_log2(n_refs) * n_refs / live_length`. With
`unk32 lifetime` the two pointers are `system` (16 refs / 122) = 5245 and
`particle` (15 refs / 86) = 5232, so `system` is allocated first and takes r7
while `particle` falls to r10 — the mirror image of the target and the cause of
all 122 differences.

`unk16 lifetime` makes `particle->unk1A = lifetime` lower through
`store_bit_field` (an existing-halfword read, an AND with a zero mask, an IOR and
the store). Two of those insns survive to `.lreg`, so `system`'s live length
becomes 124 (priority 5161 < 5232), `particle` is allocated first and gets r7,
`system` gets r10, and every register role then matches the target. This is the
single change that took the function from 122 to 38 differing instructions.

### Remaining residue and its first divergence

First divergence: target `0x88 mov r0, #0`, base `mov r6, #0`. Target 149
instructions, base 148; base is missing the target's second `mov r0, #0` at
0x114.

Mechanism, measured in the pass dumps:
- In `.rtl`, `particle->unk4 = particle->unk8 = particle->unkC = 0;` produces
  three separate const-0 pseudos (117/118/119), and the `store_bit_field` mask
  contributes a fourth.
- The first `.cse` pass merges all of them into one pseudo (insn 149,
  `(set (reg:SI 112) (const_int 0))` with `REG_EQUIV`) and also rewrites the tail
  store `(set (mem:HI [system+6]) (reg:HI 255))` into
  `(set (mem:HI …) (subreg:HI (reg:SI 112) 0))`. That donation gives the constant
  5 references across 132 insns, so it is a global allocno; it takes r6 (the only
  free LO_REG over its range) and the rest of the reload scratch choices shift.
- In the target the constant is not shared: the group of three gets one block-local
  `mov r0, #0` and the tail gets its own. Proof that this is the *only* remaining
  difference: changing the tail store to `system->unk6 = 1;` produces 149
  instructions with exactly one differing (`mov r0, #1` vs `mov r0, #0`), every
  register and scratch identical.
