# Teletype

## sub_8063F64 (0x8063F64)

- The function stores zero at offset `0`, passes the embedded `SpriteTextBlock` at offset `0xEC` to `sub_8060CDC`, then loads the `AllocatedBlock *` at offset `0xC4` and calls `deallocateBlock`.
- `TeletypeState` fields `unk0`, `unkC4`, and embedded `unkEC` are required to reproduce the target address formation and access widths.

## sub_806415C (0x806415C)

- A local prototype for `sub_8063CF4(TeletypeState *)` was sufficient to reproduce the call and literal-pool mask exactly.

## sub_806417C (0x806417C)

## sub_8064188 (0x8064188)

## sub_8063F5C (0x8063F5C)

- The function stores its callback parameter at offset `0xDC`; declaring `unkDC` as a function-pointer field reproduces the direct word store without a cast.

## Bitfield experiment for `unkBA`

- A halfword allocation unit with named one-bit fields `unkBA_0` through `unkBA_4` and an unnamed eleven-bit remainder was tested at all three sites.
- In `sub_806415C`, the first divergence was at `+0x0A`: the target uses `ldr` for the halfword mask, while the bitfield form emits `mov #5`; it then emits `negs`, `ldrb`, and a final `strb` instead of the target `ldrh` and `strh`.
- In `sub_806417C`, the first divergence was at `+0x02`: the target uses `ldrh`, while the bitfield form emits `ldrb`; the bitfield form then emits `lsl #29` and `lsr #31` instead of the target `lsr #2`.
- In `sub_8064188`, the first divergence was at `+0x02`: the target uses `ldrh`, while the bitfield form emits `ldrb`; the bitfield form then emits `lsl #30` and `lsr #31` instead of the target `lsr #1`.
- The scalar `unk16 unkBA` form is retained because named bitfield accesses do not reproduce the target whole-halfword reads and read-modify-write.

## sub_8063CF4 (0x8063CF4)

- The matching field accesses are `unkAC` and `unkB0` word stores, `unkBE` and `unkC0` byte stores, `unkBC` halfword increment/readback, `unkC8` pointer assignment from `unkEC.prev`, and a nullable callback at `unkE8` receiving the state and `unkBC`.
- `unkC8` is typed as `SpriteEntry *` so the assignment from the embedded `SpriteTextBlock`'s `prev` pointer remains a typed pointer operation; this reproduces the target's staged `+0xC0`, `+0x2C` address formation.
- The callback field is typed as `void (*)(struct TeletypeState *, unk16)`; the target's `ldrh` argument load and indirect call match directly.

## sub_8063E18 (0x8063E18)

- The constructor remains parked because no natural C form reproduced the target. The target is `0x128` bytes including its four-word literal pool; the best signed indexed draft also ends at `0x128` and places the pool at `0x118`. The active source is the original dump, and the typed semantic draft is retained directly above it in a bare `#if 0` block.
- The constructor scratch layout includes `unk0` at `0x0`, `unk4` at `0x4`, and `pad08[9]` through `0x10`; `unk11` and every later field therefore retain their proven offsets. The live `TeletypeState` header does not expose `unk4` because no committed matched C accesses it.
- The draft uses `AllocatedBlock *block`, `TeletypeStateDraft *state`, `s32 i`, and `SpriteEntry *sprite`; it calls `slowAllocate(0xF8)`, populates the recovered fields, initializes the embedded `SpriteTextBlock`, loads the sprite chain, installs four callbacks, copies `arg1[0xA]` into eight bytes, and clears `unk1C[0x80]`.
- The draft was temporarily enabled with its `8063e18.s` include and public `sub_8063E18` prototype disabled in temporary copies; `CMakeFiles/rom.dir/src/teletype.c.o` built successfully. The source and header were restored with the draft parked. The update draft was enabled and tested separately under the same procedure below.

- The target's final clear loop is pointer-shaped: it materializes the base in `r1`, forms the end pointer with `add r0, #0x7F`, stores a byte, decrements the pointer, and compares the pointer against the base with signed `bge`. The indexed drafts use a counter/array-index loop with a strength-reduced address and compare the index against zero; they retain `bge` but do not match the target CFG or instruction sequence.
- `unkB8` is a distinct halfword field: the constructor stores `arg6` there, while `unkBA` is separately zeroed and is accessed as a whole halfword by neighboring functions. The rejected `unkBA` bitfield experiment generated byte accesses (`ldrb`/`strb`) where the target uses `ldrh`/`strh`.
- The target's `ldr [sp, #0x34]; lsl #16; lsr #16` sequence is retained by the `unk16 arg6` ABI formal and proves the constructor's seventh parameter is narrow at entry. The constructor call to `sub_8060C1C` uses the already-narrow `arg2` without a cast.
- The constructor was tested in more than the coordinator's approximate forty-build budget after the signed-loop hint (55 Bash build invocations were counted in the agent execution log before the later counting commands). It is intentionally parked rather than subjected to further artificial register permutations.
- Open items are the exact natural source lifetime that assigns incoming `arg0` and normalized `arg6` pseudos to target `r4/r5`, and the natural source form that produced the pointer-cursor final clear loop. No casts, volatile qualifiers, raw offset dereferences, or artificial aliases are retained in the parked draft.

- The draft reproduces the target dispatch setup with `current = state->unk0; code = current[0]; cursor = current; switch (code)`. This emits `ldr [r4]`, `ldrb`, `mov r2`, and `mov r3` in target order. It also reproduces the target spacing update and parser callback flow.
- Reversing the byte-coordinate operands to `state->unkC0 + state->unk12 > state->unk11` was required for target load order. The target forms the `unkC0` address first, loads `unk12`, loads the byte at `unkC0`, adds in `unk12`, then compares against `unk11` with signed `ble` at function offset `+0xCC` (`0x8064050`). The prior `state->unk12 + state->unkC0` spelling loaded the operands in the opposite order.
- The target word-coordinate test loads `unkAC` through the pointer in `r7` at function offset `+0xE4` (`0x8064068`), loads `unkB4` at `+0xE6` (`0x806406A`), adds them, loads `unkA4` at `+0xEE` (`0x8064072`), and uses signed `ble` at `+0xF2` (`0x8064076`). This is signedness evidence for those operands when the tested draft declared `unkA4`, `unkAC`, and `unkB4` as `s32`; no `asr`, `ldrsh`, or `ldsb` occurs for these fields.
- The live header keeps `unkA4`, `unkAC`, and `unkB4` as padding or `unk32`-backed layout because the signed declarations are required only by parked code. `unkAC` is written as zero by matched `sub_8063CF4`; no committed matched C reads these three fields in the teletype TU.
- The final open questions are the natural loop shape and whether the separate `code`, `current`, and `cursor` locals are all required by the original source. No new experiments were run for those questions in this review.
- The target saves both high registers even though no visible body instruction consumes incoming `r9`: `mov r7, r9; mov r6, r8; push {r6, r7}`. The baseline saves only `r8`: `mov r7, r8; push {r7}`. This unresolved save-mask mismatch and its `+0x02` first divergence are why the function remains parked.
